# Consumer interview guide (25–30 min)

Who: people who go out to bars in Manhattan, ideally the Lower East Side (LES).
Goal: learn how groups pick a bar tonight, what goes wrong, and which signals
they trust. Past behavior first; the concept comes last so it does not lead the
earlier answers.

Rules for the interviewer
- Ask about the last real night out ("tell me about the last time…"). If they
  drift into "I would…", bring them back: "What did you actually do last time?"
- Don't pitch. Don't say whether an answer is "right".
- Take notes in an off-repo doc. Fill the note template
  (`note_template.md`) right after the call. No names, contacts or handles.

## Screener (2 min, before scheduling)

1. Are you 21 or older? (Must be yes.)
2. In the last 30 days, how many nights did you go out to a bar or club?
   (Qualifies: 2 or more.)
3. Which neighborhoods do you usually go out in? (Prefer LES, East Village or
   nearby; at most 2 of the 7 consumer slots from outside the LES area.)
4. Do you usually go out with a group of 2 or more people? (Prefer yes.)
5. Do you work at a bar, or for a nightlife or venue-tech company? (If yes, do
   not count as a consumer; route to the operator guide if they qualify there.)

Qualifies: 21+, 2+ nights out in the last 30 days, not nightlife industry.
Mix: try to get at least 2 people who usually pick for their group and 2 who
usually don't.

## Consent script (1 min, verbal)

> Thanks for doing this. It's for a class project at NYU about how people pick
> bars on a night out. It takes about 25 minutes. I'll take notes, but I won't
> record your name, phone number, or social handles, and notes are anonymized
> with an ID number. You can skip any question or stop at any time, no problem.
> There's no payment or gift for this; we don't have a budget, so we really
> appreciate your time. Is it OK to go ahead?

Proceed only on a clear yes. Write "consent: verbal yes" in your off-repo notes.

## Warm-up (3 min)

W1. Roughly how many nights in the last 30 days did you go out to a bar or
    club?
W2. Who do you usually go out with, and how many of you are there?
W3. When your group goes out, who usually picks the place? Tell me how it
    went the last time. `[H9]`
    - Probe: Was that the same person as the time before? Does it rotate?
    - Probe: Did anyone push back on the pick?

## Core questions (12–14 min)

1. Think about the last time you went to a bar that turned out to be wrong for
   the night. What happened? `[H1]`
   - Probe: Wrong how: too empty, too packed, long line, wrong music, a cover
     you didn't expect?
   - Probe: When was that? In the last month?
2. What did you do when you found out? Did you stay, leave, or go somewhere
   else? `[H1]`
   - Probe: How long did that take? What did it cost you (cover, Uber, time)?
3. How often does that happen? Tell me about another time if one comes to
   mind. `[H1]`
   - Probe: Of your last few nights out, how many went as planned?
4. Walk me through the last time your group decided where to go. Start from
   the first message. `[H2] [H9]`
   - Probe: About how many minutes from "where should we go?" to a decision?
   - Probe: Where did it happen: group chat, in person, on the street?
5. What apps or sources did you look at to decide that night? Show me if you
   can. `[H3]`
   - Probe: Google Maps, Instagram stories, group chat, Partiful, Resy, DICE,
     Yelp, TikTok? Anything else?
   - Probe: What did each one tell you?
6. What did you want to know that none of those told you? `[H3]`
   - Probe: Did you ever text someone already at a bar to ask what it's like?
7. Last time you saw info about how busy a bar was (Google "live" busyness, an
   Instagram story, a friend's text), how old was it? Did you trust it? `[H4]`
   - Probe: At what point would it have felt too old to act on? 15 min, 30,
     an hour, two hours?
8. Last time you picked between two or three bars, what tipped it? `[H5]`
   - Probe: Music, line, how crowded, dancing, cover, distance, drink prices,
     friends being there?
   - Probe: If you had to name the top three, in order?
   - Probe: What was a deal-breaker?

## Concept test (6–8 min)

Default: read the description below aloud. If frontend mockup PNGs exist
(check the frontend workstream folder before the interview), show them on your
phone after reading it, and note which screen they reacted to.

> Picture an app for picking a bar right now. People already at bars, called
> Scouts, send a 10-second "vibe check": how crowded it is from 1 to 5, whether
> people are dancing, how long the line is, the cover, and what music is on.
> Bars can also post official updates like cover, DJ or drink offers. For each
> bar, the app shows a vibe score, how confident it is, and how fresh the info
> is, like "12 min ago". Your group sets what you want, and it ranks the bars
> nearby. Scouts earn points they can trade for perks.

Scenario: "It's Saturday, Nov 14, 11:30 pm. You and 3 friends are on Ludlow
St. You're deciding where to go next."

1. First reaction: what would you do with this in that moment? What's
   confusing? `[H12]`
   - Probe: On a scale of 1 to 5, from very negative to very positive, where
     are you? Why not one higher?
2. You see two reports on the same bar: one from the venue saying "no line",
   one from 3 Scouts 10 minutes ago saying "20-minute line". Which do you
   believe, and why? `[H6] [H4]`
   - Probe: What would make you trust a report more: how recent it is, how
     many people sent it, a verified Scout badge, or the venue's own post?
3. Would you send a 10-second vibe check yourself from a bar you're at? Have
   you ever done something like it (a story, a text to friends)? `[H7]`
   - Probe: What would get you to do it: points, perks, status, helping
     friends, nothing?
4. Think about the last month. On which of your nights out would you have
   opened this? `[H12]`
   - Probe: Would it have changed where you went? Would you recommend it to
     the friend who usually picks?

## Wrap-up (2 min)

- "Anything I should have asked but didn't?"
- "Is there anyone you know, who goes out in the LES, who might talk to us?
  Please pass on our message; don't share their contact with me in notes."
- Thank them. Remind them notes are anonymized and nothing identifies them.
